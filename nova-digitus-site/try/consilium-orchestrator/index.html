The problem

Assistants answer. Operators notice.

A chatbot is a good employee who never leaves their desk and never speaks first. The owner still has to know what to ask, and knowing what to ask is most of the job.

Consilium inverts that. The specialists run continuously against your connected systems, looking for the four things that actually move a small business: money that is late, demand that is moving, time that is being wasted, and customers who are going quiet. When one of them finds something, it does the work — drafts the email, builds the schedule, prices the scenario — and then stops at the last inch and hands it to you.

The product is that last inch. Everything else is table stakes.

Design constraint that shapes the whole system: the owner is holding a phone, standing up, with about twenty seconds. If a proposal cannot be understood and decided in twenty seconds, the agent has not finished its work.

Orchestration model

The loop every proposal runs

This is a real sequence, and the order matters — an agent that proposes before it drafts is just a notification, and a notification is more work, not less.

  1. 01SenseSpecialists poll connected systems on their own cadence.
  2. 02QualifyConsilium scores signal against your goals and kills the noise.
  3. 03DraftThe agent does the work fully — copy, numbers, calendar slots.
  4. 04ProposeOne card. What, why, what changes if you say yes.
  5. 05You decideApprove, edit, decline. Nothing moves before this.
  6. 06Execute & verifyAgent acts, confirms it landed, writes the audit record.

Decline feeds back into step 02. Three declines on the same trigger and Consilium stops raising it, then tells you it stopped.

How Consilium decides what reaches you

Every candidate is scored on four axes before it becomes a card. A candidate that clears the bar on none of them is logged and never shown.

ConfidenceIs the underlying data clean enough to act on?Below threshold — agent asks for data, not a decision

Conflict resolution

Two agents will eventually want opposite things — Echo wants to discount the slow mover, Sentinel wants the margin. Consilium does not average them. It surfaces the disagreement as a single card with both positions and the number that separates them, because that is exactly the decision an owner should be making and an agent should not.

Human-in-the-loop

The approval contract

Autonomy is configured per action class, not per agent. An agent you trust with one thing is not automatically trusted with another, and the tier can move in both directions as evidence accumulates.

TierWhat the agent may doYour involvementUndo
ObserveRead and summarize. No writes anywhere.NoneN/A
ProposeDraft fully, then stop. Default for everything.One tapN/A — nothing happened
NoticeAct, then send you a receipt.Read the receipt15-minute window
ApproveDraft, then stop. Cannot be lowered to Notice.Explicit approval, alwaysN/A
DualDraft, then stop. Two named approvers.Two peopleN/A

Hard gates

Six action classes can never be configured below approve, regardless of tier settings, trust history, or how many times you have approved the same thing. This is a property of the platform, not a preference.

  • Money out — any payment, refund, or transfer
  • Price changes — anything a customer will see
  • Bulk external send — more than 25 recipients
  • Contract signature — including e-sign envelopes
  • Data deletion — in any connected system
  • New commitments to customers — dates, scopes, promises

What the audit record holds

Every executed action writes an immutable entry: the triggering signal and its timestamp, the agent, the exact tier that permitted it, the approver, the payload as sent, and the verification result. Ninety days on the base tier, seven years on regulated packs. This is what makes the Financial Services pack possible at all.

Proactive orchestration

Six ways a card gets born

"Proactive" is not a personality trait, it is a trigger taxonomy. These six cover nearly everything a small business actually needs caught.

TriggerFires onWorked example
ThresholdA number crosses a line you setRunway drops under 8 weeks
AnomalyA number leaves its own normal rangeTuesday refunds triple the trailing median
TemporalA date approachesQuarterly estimate due in 14 days
SilenceAn expected event does not happenYour best repeat customer hasn't ordered in 40 days
CorrelationTwo unrelated systems agreeCalendar gap + a nine-day-old estimate request
ExternalSignal from outside your wallsHeat wave forecast against your patio inventory

Silence triggers are the ones owners consistently say they'd never have caught. They're also the hardest to tune, because absence has no timestamp.

Configuration

The whole platform in one file

Configuration is declarative and versioned. The mobile settings screens are a view onto this document — every toggle writes here, and every change is diffable.

consilium.config.yamltenant / smb-base
# ── orchestrator ──────────────────────────────────────────────
orchestrator:
  name: consilium
  timezone: "America/Chicago"
  surface: mobile_first
  cadence:
    brief: "07:15"            # one card stack, ranked
    sweep: "*/30 08:00-18:00"  # business-hours signal pass
    weekly: "FRI 16:00"       # batched, non-decaying items
  quiet_hours: ["21:00", "06:30"]   # hard gates still break through
  max_cards_per_day: 6            # overflow batches to weekly

# ── autonomy ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
autonomy:
  default_tier: propose          # observe|propose|notice|approve|dual
  hard_gates:                    # cannot be lowered, ever
    - money_out
    - price_change
    - external_send: { recipients_over: 25 }
    - contract_signature
    - data_deletion
    - customer_commitment
  dual_approval:
    - money_out: { amount_over: 2500 }
    - vendor_contract
  undo_window_minutes: 15         # applies to tier: notice only
  trust_promotion:
    enabled: true
    rule: "20 consecutive approvals, zero edits, 30 days"
    promotes_to: notice           # never past a hard gate

# ── agents ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
agents:
  echo:     { domain: marketing,  tier: propose, voice: owner_profile }
  vortex:   { domain: sales,      tier: propose, rank_by: intent_signal }
  atlas:    { domain: scheduling, tier: propose, buffer_min: 45 }
  sentinel: { domain: finance,    tier: approve, runway_alert_weeks: 8 }
  spark:    { domain: creative,   tier: observe }
  sage:     { domain: knowledge,  tier: observe, cite: always }
  cyber:    { domain: security,   tier: approve }
  forge:    { domain: discovery,  tier: propose, budget: 1_card_per_week }
  rig:      { domain: integrations, tier: notice }

# ── triggers ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
triggers:
  silence:
    repeat_customer_dormant_days: 40
    quote_unanswered_days: 5
  threshold:
    invoice_age_days: 28
    cash_runway_weeks: 8
  anomaly:
    sensitivity: medium         # low|medium|high — tunes false positives
  suppression:
    decline_streak_to_mute: 3   # then Consilium tells you it muted it

# ── vertical pack ─────────────────────────────────────────────
pack: retail                     # retail | financial_services | industrial
retention:
  audit_log_days: 90             # 2555 on financial_services
  decision_rationale: retained   # required for regulated packs

Vertical packs

Not just AI. Vertical AI, built for real work — and the real world.

The base platform is the same. A pack changes what gets sensed, which agents come online, what the hard gates cover, and how long the record is kept. Depth beats breadth: a general agent that knows a little about retail is worth less than one that knows what a shelf gap costs on a Saturday.

Retail

pack / retail

Built for retail's full demand chain.

High-velocity retailers lose money in three places at once: the wrong price, the missing unit, and the shelf nobody fixed. The pack reads shopper signal, forecasts demand at the store-and-SKU level, and tunes inventory and promotion together instead of one at a time — store by store, not chain-wide.

Signals in

  • POS velocity & basket mix
  • Shelf photos and planogram drift
  • Local weather and event calendars
  • Competitor price feeds

Agents on duty

  • Vortex — demand & pricing
  • Rig — inventory sync
  • Echo — promotion timing
  • Forge — assortment gaps

Pack gates

  • Price change — always Approve
  • Markdown depth over 20%
  • Cross-store transfers

Example proactive moveStore 4 goes out of stock on the 12-pack in three days at current velocity, and the competitor two blocks over is already out. Consilium proposes: hold price, pull 40 units from Store 7 where it's slow, feature it in Thursday's email. Margin impact modeled, transfer paperwork drafted. One tap.

Industrial

pack / industrial

When output drops, our AI knows why.

Every minute of downtime hits margin, flow and delivery. The pack correlates across assets, lines and sites to find the actual cause rather than the loudest alarm, then puts the fix in the hands of whoever is closest to it. Real-time root cause, from control room to connected worker.

Signals in

  • PLC / SCADA tag streams
  • Vibration & current draw
  • OEE and throughput by line
  • Work orders and shift notes

Agents on duty

  • Rig — asset telemetry
  • Forge — root cause correlation
  • Atlas — crew dispatch
  • Sentinel — margin impact

Pack gates

Example proactive moveLine 2 throughput fell 11% at 04:20. Consilium correlates it to infeed motor current drift that started Tuesday afternoon — not the temperature alarm that's been screaming since 03:00. Proposes a work order, a reroute through Line 4 for the shift, and pushes the six repair steps to the connected worker's tablet. Estimated recovery: 40 minutes.

Trust posture

How trust is earned, and how it's lost

Trust promotion is a feature, not a default. An agent can move from propose to notice on a specific action class after twenty consecutive approvals with zero edits over at least thirty days — and it moves straight back down on a single decline.

Three things the platform will not do

  1. Act during quiet hours on anything reversible. If it can wait until 6:30, it waits.
  2. Speak as you without showing you the words. Every outbound draft is displayed in full before send, not summarized.
  3. Hide a decline. Declined proposals stay in the log with your reason, and the pattern is shown back to you monthly — that's how the system learns your business rather than your clicks.

Onboarding sequence

Two connectors, one proven recipe, then a business interview. The first week is deliberately narrow: Sentinel on invoice aging and Vortex on quote silence, both on propose. Those two produce visible money fastest and have the cleanest ground truth, which means trust is built on things the owner can verify with their own eyes before anything harder comes online.
