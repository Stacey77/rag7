How the money works

Every number a seller needs, printed before they sign.

The single fastest way to lose a first-time seller is a surprise. Every product in the catalogue shows its price and the seller's share on the card itself, and the Money tab reconciles what actually landed. Nothing is folded into "handling".

LineWho sets itWhat the seller sees
Product priceSeller, within a floor and ceilingEditable on the product card. Changing it updates the projected share instantly.
Seller shareYou, per productPrinted as a percentage and a dollar figure on every card. Ranges 50–70% depending on the product.
Payment processingThe processorShown as its own line on the Money tab, not silently absorbed.
Ad spendSellerTracked against the starting credit and then against their own card. Never auto-charged without a confirmation.
Payout timingYouStated on the Money tab in days, and held to.
Five things to settle before this takes a real payment

1 · Ad credits. "$40 free to start" is a promotional credit from an ad platform, and those come with conditions — usually a matching spend requirement, an eligibility window, and new-account-only rules. Print the actual terms next to the offer or it becomes a complaint. This prototype shows a generic starting credit for that reason.

2 · Income claims. Never show an earnings figure a seller has not made. In the US, income and business-opportunity claims are regulated territory, and "sellers earn $3,000/month" needs substantiation you almost certainly do not have. The Money tab here shows real activity only, and says so when it is empty.

3 · Resale rights. Every digital product you supply needs a written licence permitting resale under the seller's brand. Get it in writing from whoever produces them, and keep it on file.

4 · The growth manager is a payroll line. "A real person available by text" is a staffing commitment, not a feature flag. Decide the ratio of managers to sellers and the response-time promise before you advertise it — and if it is ever AI-assisted, say so.

5 · Refunds on instant delivery. Digital goods delivered immediately still need a refund policy, and card networks will enforce one whether you have written it or not. Write it first.
